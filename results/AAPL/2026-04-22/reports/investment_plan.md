I align with the bear case, so the recommendation is Sell.

The bull made the strongest possible version of Apple’s quality story: enormous free cash flow, elite margins, a massive installed base, relentless buybacks, and a still-intact ecosystem. Those points are real. Apple is clearly not operationally broken, and the succession plan sounds orderly rather than chaotic. The bull also makes a fair point that Apple does not need to win AI infrastructure to benefit from AI, and that a product-focused CEO could eventually help reset the narrative.

But the bear argument is more compelling for the stock at this price. The core issue is not whether Apple is a great company. It is whether the current stock still offers attractive risk-adjusted upside when it trades at roughly 33.7x trailing earnings and 28.4x forward earnings, while the next growth leg is still mostly a projection. The most convincing bear point is that investors are paying a premium now for AI/device-layer monetization, leadership renewal, and product acceleration that have not yet been demonstrated. That matters because premium multiples compress quickly when execution shifts from assumed to needing proof.

The other reason I side with the bear is that several risks are converging at once instead of appearing in isolation. Apple is navigating an AI credibility gap, a CEO transition, continuing China exposure, and regulatory pressure on services, all while the chart is sitting near a fragile support area after a sharp reversal from a fast rebound. The bull treats the `266` level as a normal retest. The bear is right that this actually highlights how conditional the setup is. If the thesis depends on a narrow technical hold while the fundamental narrative is still unproven, that is not the kind of asymmetry I want to buy.

The lesson to carry forward from past mistakes is this: do not confuse business durability with stock attractiveness, and do not let optionality stand in for execution. In similar situations, the error is often giving too much credit to a world-class franchise just because it has historically earned the benefit of the doubt. This time the market is asking for evidence, not reputation. Until that evidence shows up in a clearer AI roadmap, stronger product-cycle proof, or a valuation reset, caution is warranted.

So the actionable call is Sell, or if the trader does not currently own it, avoid initiating here.

The investment plan is straightforward. If the trader already holds Apple as a tactical position, reduce or exit the position into current strength rather than waiting for the market to answer the `266` question for you. If `266` breaks decisively, that likely opens downside toward the `258.8` to `260.5` zone, and below that toward `253.5` to `256`. There is no reason to sit through that if the upside case is still based on hoped-for narrative improvement. If the trader has a long-term core allocation and tax constraints matter, I would still trim meaningfully and keep only a smaller residual position until management proves the next chapter deserves a premium multiple.

If the trader does not own the stock, I would stay out for now. I would only reconsider if one of two things happens: either valuation becomes materially more forgiving after a pullback, or Apple delivers concrete evidence that AI and product execution are moving from concept to monetizable reality. In other words, wait for better price or better proof. Right now you have neither.

For implementation, I would do three things. First, sell down exposure immediately rather than averaging in near contested support. Second, set a strict re-entry framework tied to evidence, not brand loyalty: a clearer AI/product roadmap, better confirmation from earnings or developer announcements, and a chart that reclaims resistance rather than barely defending support. Third, redeploy capital into names where growth drivers are already visible and valuation is better aligned with execution.

Bottom line: the bull case explains why Apple remains a great company. The bear case better explains why the stock is vulnerable from here. That is why the right call in this debate is Sell.
